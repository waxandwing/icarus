Product decisions should be judged against the rules below, not against whichever prototype happens to be newest.


SOURCE BRAND TRUTH
Wax & Wing direction: modern editorial utility with analog artifacts.
Audience: experienced teachers and other teachers with established ways of working; never administrator-first and never child-app aesthetics.
Arc expression ratio: approximately 80% structure / 20% expression.
Arc is the most restrained product in the family because the calendar and teacher work must remain visually dominant.
Cactus Farm is outside this brand system.


THE ARC CONSTRUCTION RULES


1. THE CALENDAR IS THE CENTER
The open-planner/calendar surface is the visual truth. Furniture attaches beyond its edges; it never overlays, narrows, or visually demotes the calendar..
Arc is calendar-first. Year, quarter, month, week, and day are different scales of the same teaching truth, not separate mini-products. The Arc mark returns the teacher to their primary calendar context. Dashboards, feeds, AI panels, widgets, and side tools may support the calendar but may not replace it as the center of gravity.


Test: If the calendar disappeared, would the screen still look like the product? If yes, we may be drifting away from Arc.


2. ARC HOLDS CONTINUITY, NOT JUST PLANS.
The product must preserve the relationship between what was intended, what actually happened, what changed, and what happens next. Planning, live teaching, interruption, recovery, and later reuse are one connected system.


Core loop: capture → sort → place → teach → adjust → recover → reuse.


3. NOTHING IMPORTANT DISAPPEARS SILENTLY.
Moving, shifting, resizing, importing, colliding, or recovering work must never cause silent loss. Fixed dates remain fixed unless the teacher explicitly changes them. Conflicts are surfaced before consequence. Destructive changes require clear intent and reliable undo/history.


This is a trust rule, not merely an error-handling rule.


4. THE TEACHER REMAINS THE AUTHORITY.
Arc assists with the infrastructure around teaching. It does not posture as the expert in the room. Automation and AI must be optional, contextual, inspectable, and reversible. Arc may suggest; the teacher decides.


The product should feel like: “I already know how to teach. This helps me keep hold of everything around it.”


5. RECOVERY MATTERS AS MUCH AS PLANNING.
School schedules fail constantly. Assemblies, testing, absences, weather, pacing differences, partial lessons, and class-specific interruptions are normal states, not edge cases. Arc should make recovery from disruption unusually safe and easy.


A shift is not merely a date operation. Arc should understand what was completed, partial, missed, fixed, or affected downstream.


6. ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH ACROSS SCALES.
A lesson moved in Week must be moved in Month, Quarter, Year, Day, and Live Classroom. A unit extended in Month must remain extended everywhere else. No view may maintain its own conflicting copy of the schedule.


Views are lenses over shared state.


7. LIVE CLASSROOM IS THE TEACHING SURFACE OF ARC.
Live Classroom should not become a disconnected widget product or an arms race with Classroomscreen. Its advantage is continuity: the lesson already planned in Arc becomes the live classroom surface with almost no re-entry.


Arc asks: What am I teaching and when?
Live Classroom asks: What do students need right now?
Live Classroom should return useful teaching-state information back to Arc.


8. SUPPORT EXISTING TOOLS; DO NOT REBUILD THE INTERNET.
Arc should connect the teacher’s files, presentations, LMS links, calendars, resources, and other tools to the right teaching context. It should not attempt to replace Google Drive, Canva, the LMS, or every classroom utility merely to claim “all in one.”


Arc’s value is knowing how those things relate to the teacher’s plan.


9. DIRECT MANIPULATION SHOULD FEEL PHYSICAL, NOT TOY-LIKE.
Lessons, units, notes, and Ideas can feel placed, shifted, stretched, tucked, pinned, or carried forward. This gives teachers spatial intuition without pretending the screen is a literal desk.


Dragging is an enhancement, never the only path. Every meaningful drag interaction requires a keyboard/click/touch alternative.


10. QUIET STRUCTURE, CONTROLLED EXPRESSION.
Arc is 80% structured / 20% expressive. Visual personality belongs in selective marks, paper-cut edges, annotations, warm color, texture, and moments of identity. Dense work surfaces—especially the calendar—stay calmer.


Do not decorate every component. Expression should increase recognition or pleasure without reducing scan speed.


11. ANALOG MEMORY, NOT SKEUOMORPHISM.
Use matte thin-paper feeling, restrained torn/clipped shapes, dry oil-pastel or wax-crayon marks, real/credible handwriting accents, and slight imperfection. Do not create a literal desk scene, thick stacked paper, glossy tape, clay/plastic icons, photoreal materials, or nostalgia as costume.


The goal is the emotional memory of excellent school supplies and grown-up stationery, filtered through contemporary interface design.


12. COLOR ORIENTS BEFORE IT DECORATES.
Default field: Paper #F3EBDD.
Default text: Ink #2C2E2E.
Signature warm anchors: Mustard #E4B33D and Terracotta #C96845.
Principal cool counterweight: Dusty Blue #7C9CAD.
Support: Sage #9AAA89, Dusty Pink #C99A9E, Lavender #AAA0B8, Kraft #C6A77D, Soft Charcoal #4A4945.


Do not assign every feature a loud identifying color. Selection/state cannot rely on color alone.


13. DIGITAL GEOMETRY STAYS DISCIPLINED.
Use a 4 px spacing base and the 4/8/12/16/24/32/48/64 scale. Default panel/card padding 24 px; dense utility padding 16 px. Default radius 8 px, small 4 px, large 12 px.


Avoid pillification and all-rounded-everything. Expressive containers may use torn edges, clips, arches, tabs, notches, tags, or asymmetrical rectangles, but use one expressive shape family per local composition.


14. TYPOGRAPHY MUST FEEL ADULT AND EDITORIAL.
Primary UI: Nunito Sans or Avenir Next Rounded, 400/600/700. Rounded is acceptable; bubbly is not.
Editorial serif: Georgia, Source Serif 4, or Newsreader for selected statements/headings, never primary navigation.
Handwriting: accents, notes, underlines, and brief asides only. Never essential navigation, long copy, form fields, or accessibility-critical information.


Typography is reviewed visually. Kerning and line breaks are not accepted blindly.


15. FORMS AND CORE CONTROLS ARE ALLOWED TO BE BORING.
Data entry, settings, imports, account management, and other high-cognitive-load utility areas should be calm, obvious, and generous. Brand personality may frame these surfaces but must not make them harder to operate.


16. MOTION EXPLAINS CAUSE AND EFFECT.
Motion should be short, physical, and legible: slide, shift, tuck, fold, pin, draw. Avoid bounce, sparkle, celebration motion, game-like reward loops, and animation that blocks understanding.


Respect reduced-motion settings. During consequential transitions, temporary interaction locks are acceptable when they prevent accidental conflicting actions, but the interface must clearly remain responsive and understandable.


17. ACCESSIBILITY IS PART OF THE INTERACTION MODEL.
Minimum body copy 16 px in product UI; 14 px only for secondary metadata with strong contrast. Essential controls/copy meet WCAG AA. Do not rely on texture, handwriting, color, hover, or dragging to communicate essential information. Keyboard-only, zoom/small-laptop, touch, contrast, and reduced-motion use are release requirements, not cleanup work.


18. THE PRODUCT MUST WORK WHEN TIRED.
A teacher should understand the next relevant action within roughly five seconds. Arc cannot require users to remember its own organizational system before it can help them remember theirs.


This is the test for hierarchy, labels, onboarding, recovery, menus, and empty states.


19. LANGUAGE SOUNDS LIKE A CAPABLE COLLEAGUE.
No corporate ed-tech language, productivity clichés, motivational coaching, childish teacher-cute phrasing, or AI-coded filler. Avoid “streamline your workflow,” “unlock,” “empower,” “transform your teaching,” “smart planning,” and equivalent generic claims.


Use concrete language about the teacher’s actual work: move, continue, shift, fix, hold, place, teach, review, return.


20. ARC SHOULD NOT REWARD BUSYNESS.
No streaks, productivity scores, guilt mechanics, gamified completion, or cheerful scolding when plans change. The product should reduce cognitive load and unfinishedness, not turn teaching into another performance dashboard.


21. VISUAL FUNCTION WINS EVERY TIE.
The screen must remain fully understandable if decorative texture disappears. Functional icons read at 20–24 px. Texture becomes more appropriate around 32 px and above. Navigation uses clean geometry. Hand-drawn marks may support attention but never reduce target clarity.


22. BUILD THE SYSTEM, NOT THE CURRENT MOCKUP.
No prototype, deployment, screenshot, or historical branch is itself canonical simply because it is newer. Preserve product truth, state logic, brand rules, and successful interaction patterns independently from deployments. A new implementation must prove equivalence before replacing a known-good behavior.


23. FEATURES MUST EARN THEIR PLACE.
Before adding a feature, ask:
• Does it help the teacher know where they are?
• Does it help them understand what changed?
• Does it help them know what happens next?
• Does it preserve teaching continuity or reduce cognitive load?


If the answer is no to all four, the feature probably does not belong in Arc.


24. RELEASES ARE JUDGED BY TRUST, NOT FEATURE COUNT.
Every release should pass these questions:
• Does this feel made for an adult teacher rather than sold to a district?
• Is the calendar/task/classroom function obvious before decoration?
• Could the screen work without texture?
• Are there too many rounded rectangles?
• Did nostalgia become costume?
• Does anything look glossy, plasticky, clay-like, generic-edtech, or obviously AI-generated?
• Would a tired teacher understand the next action quickly?
• Can a keyboard-only user complete the same meaningful work?
• Does save → reload preserve state?
• Does movement preserve history and avoid silent loss?
• Do all calendar scales and Live Classroom show the same underlying truth?


WORKING PRODUCT DEFINITION
Arc is a calendar-centered teaching workspace that helps a teacher know where they are, what changed, and what happens next. It holds continuity between planning, live teaching, disruption, recovery, and later reuse while leaving the teaching decisions to the teacher.


SHORTEST INTERNAL TEST
Arc holds your place.


If a future design or feature violates these rules, the default response is to change the design or feature—not to weaken the rule to accommodate the implementation.


25. DOCUMENT THE BUILD AS IT HAPPENS.
Arc maintains two recoverable records of major build decisions: the canonical Google Drive documents and an in-chat checkpoint. Product, architecture, deployment, interaction, brand, testing, and scope decisions that materially change the build must be reflected in the relevant Drive authority as they are made, not reconstructed later from memory.


Drive is the durable working record. Chat is the running backup and decision trail. Git is the implementation record. None of the three replaces the others.


Documentation must remain lean: update an existing canonical document when one exists; create a new document only when it has a distinct enduring purpose. Superseded working documents should be clearly archived rather than allowed to compete with current authority.






ARC DESKTOP / SETUP VISUAL ADDENDUM — 2026-09-04


This addendum governs perceptual execution only. Behavioral setup/import rules remain in the ARC Canonical Product Spec.


• Desk/background is an environmental surface distinct from the planner. The calendar itself remains Arc paper white in every calendar horizon.
• The calendar should read as a physical paper planner resting on that surface through restrained drop shadow and subtle faux page-edge/page-stack depth. Tactile, matte, high-end stationery; never glossy 3D or novelty skeuomorphism.
• Colored paper cutouts and hand-drawn accents may carry brand personality around Fridge, Drawer, School Notes, Personal, onboarding, and selected planner marks. They must remain restrained and cannot replace crisp core controls or accessibility semantics.
• Preferred hand-drawn replacement candidates include the red-circle mark, cross-out stroke, Fridge artifact accents, loose magnet/paper silhouettes, clip/tack cues, Drawer/Basket cue, Voice accent, Take a Look Around welcome artwork, restrained empty-state artwork, School Notes marks, Personal marker, and an optional Clean Up cue.
• Progressive setup must feel embedded in the Arc desktop, not like a generic SaaS onboarding modal/wizard. Use an integrated tray, rail, paper sheet, or similarly native Arc treatment while keeping the actual calendar visible.
• School-calendar import review should use a miniature Arc paper-white calendar as the primary visual proof. It should feel like the same planner at a smaller scale, not a separate dashboard/report component.
• Paper texture, page depth, cutout irregularity, and shadow are supporting cues. They must not reduce legibility, contrast, target clarity, responsive behavior, or high-zoom accessibility.
• Tactile direction is intentionally bounded: wonderfully tactile, not a craft-store explosion.
